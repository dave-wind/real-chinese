Your lesson

Why these three?

They unlock the post and comments, appear often in everyday online Chinese, and are useful beyond this one example.

踩雷
cǎi léi · verb
internet speech

To choose or try something that turns out to be disappointing, unreliable or poor quality.

Worth learning: common in reviews, shopping, restaurants and entertainment.
拿捏
ná niē · verb
playful slang

To handle, understand or control something with confidence. Online it often sounds playful or teasing.

In this comment: the owner knows how to attract online attention, even if the product is bad.
一言难尽
yī yán nán jìn · idiom
colloquial

There is too much to explain in a few words—usually because a situation is complicated, awkward or bad.

Natural use: a softer, more expressive alternative to simply saying 很差.
Natural translation

“This place looked really popular online, but after going once, I realized I’d fallen for the hype and made a terrible choice.”

The post is not only saying the shop was bad. It implies that its online popularity created expectations that the real experience failed to meet.

Tone and culture

The creator uses self-mocking language rather than making a formal complaint. The comments join the joke and criticize the shop’s ability to market itself.

sarcasm “太会拿捏流量了” sounds like praise on the surface, but here it criticizes the gap between marketing and reality.
